8-Ball Doubles Rules

Each game follows 8-ball rules with the league clarifications below. Teammates alternate turns during doubles play.

Object of the game

Each team is assigned solids (1–7) or stripes (9–15). A team wins by legally pocketing all balls in its group and then legally pocketing the 8-ball in a properly called pocket.

Racking

Rack all 15 object balls as tightly as possible, with the front ball on the foot spot and the 8-ball in the center.

Break

The first breaking team is decided by a coin toss or rock-paper-scissors. In later games of the same matchup, the team that won the previous game chooses which team breaks. The break must be taken from behind the head string and the rack must be struck first. If four object balls do not contact a rail, re-rack and the same player gets another attempt. If the second attempt is also illegal, the opposing team breaks.

8-ball on the break

Pocketing the 8-ball on the break is an automatic win for the breaking team, unless the cue ball is also pocketed. Then the opposing team wins.

Open table

The table stays open until a player legally pockets a ball that establishes solids or stripes. On an open table, either group may be hit first, but the 8-ball may not be hit first.

Slop

Slop rules apply. Players do not need to call every shot or pocket in advance. The pocket must be called for the 8-ball.

Fouls and ball-in-hand

A scratch, failing to hit the correct object ball first, failing to drive any ball to a rail after legal contact (unless a ball is pocketed), cue-ball movement, or a double hit gives the opposing team ball-in-hand. On a break scratch, the opposing team gets ball-in-hand behind the head string. On other fouls, ball-in-hand may be placed anywhere on the table.

8-ball loss conditions

A team loses if it pockets the 8-ball before clearing its group, pockets it in the wrong pocket, fails to properly call the pocket and makes the shot, or pockets the 8-ball on the same shot as a cue-ball scratch.

Jump and masse shots

Jump shots and masse shots are allowed.

Balls leaving the table

Pocketed balls must stay in the pocket to count. If an object ball leaves the playing surface, it is spotted as close as possible to the foot spot. Knocking the 8-ball off the table is a loss of game. A cue ball leaving the table is a scratch.

Coaching

Coaching is limited to the two players currently playing. Only the two players at the table may mark or point to a location on the table. After a warning, improper coaching gives the opposing team ball-in-hand.

Shooting position and table care

At least one foot must stay on the floor while shooting. Players may not sit on the table or mark, wet, or otherwise alter the cloth.
